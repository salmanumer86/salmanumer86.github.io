I build outsourcing partnerships between companies abroad and engineering teams in Pakistan. The same questions come up in almost every first call, so here are the honest answers.

## The talent is real, but it's not evenly distributed

Pakistan produces a large number of computer science graduates every year, and the React / Next.js / React Native ecosystem in Lahore, Karachi and Islamabad is genuinely strong. It's also uneven: the gap between a developer who has shipped production apps and one who has completed a bootcamp is wide, and both will have "React" on the CV.

That's why sourcing matters more here than in a market with a tighter distribution. You need someone reading the repo, not the keywords.

## Time zones work in your favour more than you'd think

Pakistan is UTC+5.

- **UK / Europe:** 4–5 hours of overlap every afternoon. Comfortable.
- **US East Coast:** 2–3 hours of overlap in the morning. Enough for a standup and a handover.
- **US West Coast:** Very little live overlap. This is where async discipline matters — written specs, recorded demos, clear tickets.

Most of the teams I place work a slightly shifted day to widen the overlap. Ask for it up front; don't assume.

## Rates

I'm not going to publish a rate card because it depends on seniority, stack, and whether you're hiring one person or a team. What I'll say is that the value is in the *middle* of the market, not the bottom. The cheapest option is cheap for a reason, and it usually costs more by month three.

## The mistakes I see in the first month

1. **No single point of contact on your side.** Remote teams stall when three people give conflicting priorities.
2. **Treating the first sprint as a test.** It *is* a test, but say so. Give a scoped, real piece of work with a clear definition of done.
3. **Skipping the video calls.** Written English is often stronger than spoken English at first. Two short calls a week fixes that faster than anything else.
4. **Hiring a single developer for a team-sized job.** A one-person "team" has no code review. Start with two.
